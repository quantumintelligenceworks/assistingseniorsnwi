01 We can help

Home is where people
do best.

Senior citizens thrive by staying involved in their own lives, and one of the best ways to ensure that is by enabling them to live in their homes for as long as is safely possible. But Seniors and their families shouldn’t have to handle the difficult challenges they face alone.

Assisting Seniors can help with illness or injury recovery, chronic medical conditions, changing life circumstances, and the building stresses of aging — brightening daily life through healthy cognitive, physical, and social interaction that lets Seniors hold on to their familiar routines and unique lifestyle.

02 How we help

Three kinds of help,
shaped to one person.

Most families start with a few hours a week. Care grows, shifts, or scales back as life does.

Personal Care

  • Bathing, dressing, grooming and hygiene
  • Toileting and incontinence care
  • Eating and hydration
  • Safety supervision
  • Transferring and positioning
  • Gentle exercise for strength, balance, and walking

Companionship

  • Daily routines and reminders
  • Medication reminders
  • Meal planning and preparation
  • Appointments and transportation
  • Outings and wellness activities
  • Conversation, reading, and games
  • Respite for family caregivers

Household Help

  • Dishes, dusting, vacuuming, and trash
  • Laundry and linens
  • Sorting mail and assisting with bills
  • Grocery shopping and errands
  • Prescription pickup
  • Pet care and houseplant care

Home care — not home health

We provide non-medical companionship, personal care, and household help in the home. We don’t provide skilled nursing, therapy, or medical treatment, but we work alongside home-health teams and are happy to help coordinate other resources when a need is outside our scope.

03 Our approach

Care designed
around you.

Every family’s situation is unique and often in constant motion. We can help Seniors who need only minimal services as well as those with higher-level chronic conditions such as Alzheimer’s or dementia.

When we begin designing a care plan together, we’ll discuss your needs and expectations, then fuse your ideas with our experience into a plan everyone involved approves — one that grows and changes with your wishes, so the dignity, privacy, and comfort of all are preserved.

  • Matched, not assigned Kelly knows the caregivers and she knows the families. Personality, routine, and situation all factor into who walks through the door.
  • A guest in the home Care should feel warm, not clinical. Familiar routines, familiar objects, and a home that stays the client’s own.
  • Safety, quietly Bathroom, kitchen, bedroom, stairs. Experienced eyes catch the risks a family living with them every day may not.
  • Families, kept in the loop Regular updates by phone and text — especially valuable when adult children live out of town.

06 Questions families ask

Straight answers,
no pressure.

What is non-medical home care?

Non-medical home care provides companionship, personal care assistance, meal preparation, light housekeeping, medication reminders, and transportation — support that helps Seniors live safely and comfortably at home without clinical or medical services.

How is this different from home health?

Home health involves skilled nursing, therapy, or medically supervised treatment prescribed by a physician. Assisting Seniors provides non-medical care only — we do not provide injections, wound care, skilled nursing, or medication administration. We do work alongside home-health teams and can help coordinate other resources when a need falls outside our scope.

How much care do we have to commit to?

As much or as little as the situation calls for. Some families need a few hours a week of companionship and errands; others need daily personal care, or around-the-clock support during a final stage of life. Care plans are built to grow and change with your wishes.

Can you help while a family caregiver takes a break?

Yes. Respite care is one of the most common reasons families call. Whether it is a standing afternoon each week or a few days while you travel, we can stay with your loved one so you can take care of your own life and health.

Do you provide care in assisted living or nursing facilities?

Yes. When a facility is short-staffed or a family wants dedicated one-to-one attention, we provide supplemental care alongside facility staff.

Can you help while a loved one is on hospice?

Yes. Hospice covers the medical side of end-of-life care; we cover the hours in between — companionship, personal care, meals, and the household running — so a family can keep their loved one at home. We work alongside hospice teams, including Dunes Hospice, and coordinate rather than duplicate what they provide.

What about long-term care insurance?

Some of our clients have long-term care policies, and Kelly will assist with the insurance processing and paperwork. Bring your policy to the introductory call and she will walk through it with you.

Can you help with a hospital or rehab discharge?

Yes. We work with discharge planners, social workers, and hospital case managers to coordinate home care support when a Senior is transitioning from a hospital or rehabilitation facility back home.
